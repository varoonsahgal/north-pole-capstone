Field guide · Mission 2, goal 10

Present to Santa

Teams of four or five. Eight to ten minutes. A leadership audience. Think "architecture review", not "tutorial".

What they want to hear

Your goal is not to prove that you typed Terraform correctly. Your goal is to tell the story of how you designed, built, automated and deployed a cloud application, and to show that you understand the architecture, the decisions and the problems.

Sounds like an engineering design

"We put the containers in three zones so one data centre failing does not take the app down."

Sounds like reading a tutorial

"Then we clicked Create, then we typed the name, then we clicked Next."

This outline is a framework, not a script. Change it to fit your story.

Slide by slide

1 · Title and one-sentence outcome

Keep it brutally simple. For example:

"We built a serverless gift API, put a React app in a container, deployed it to ECS with Terraform, automated delivery with CodePipeline, and extended the same pipeline to Kubernetes."

Leave out: step lists, Dockerfile screenshots, walls of Terraform.

2 · Architecture diagram

Draw your own. It should show the app, the image and registry, the API (API Gateway, Lambda, S3), the pipeline, ECS with its load balancer, EKS, and Terraform holding it together. Open with something like: "This is our architecture, end to end. We built every part of it except the React app."

3 · Roles and ownership

One sentence each: "I owned the API." "I owned the image and the pipeline." "I owned ECS and the load balancer." "I owned Terraform and shared state." Keep it crisp.

4 · The gift API

Explain the idea, not the clicks: API Gateway is the public address, Lambda holds the logic, S3 holds the data. Then tell the debugging story. Leadership loves a learning moment: what the symptom was, where you looked, what it turned out to be.

5 · The container

Talk about decisions: why two stages, how big the final image is, how the app is checked for health, how you avoided Docker Hub rate limits, how you handled CORS once the app called the API. Show three or four lines of Dockerfile at most.

6 · The pipeline

Tell the automation story: a commit starts the pipeline, CodeBuild builds and pushes the image, the deploy stages roll it out. A line that lands well: "After the first deployment, nobody on the team deployed by hand again."

7 · ECS

Why Fargate (no servers to manage). The difference between a task definition and a service. How the load balancer finds healthy containers. Why the containers accept traffic only from the load balancer.

8 · Kubernetes

Use your comparison table from goal 8. Mention the two layers of permission (IAM and Kubernetes). If you did not finish, say so plainly: "We completed the cluster and a manual deployment. What remains is the pipeline step, and here is how we would do it."

9 · Terraform

Focus on value, not file size: the state is shared and locked, there are no credentials in the repository, and you proved the code by destroying everything and rebuilding it.

10 · Demo

Thirty to sixty seconds. Show the app, change the banner, push, show the pipeline running, show the new banner. Do not scroll through logs.

11 · Challenges

Pick two or three from your captain's log. Good candidates: unhealthy targets behind the load balancer, the malformed API response, CORS, missing permissions on the build role, Terraform and the pipeline fighting over the service, getting the build into the Kubernetes cluster.

12 · What you would improve

Show that you can see further: private subnets, HTTPS with a real domain, automated tests in the pipeline, autoscaling, dashboards and alarms, Terraform applied from the pipeline with an approval step, smaller permissions for the build role.

End cleanly: "That is how we would take this to production."

🎤 Delivery tips

  • Everyone speaks once. No silent team members.
  • No slide has more than about twelve words. Diagrams beat text.
  • Appoint a timekeeper, and rehearse once with a clock running.
  • Record your demo in advance. Live demos fail at the worst moment.
  • Speak to executives: architecture, decisions, problems, value.
  • Clean up afterwards. The cleanup guide has the order.
